Knowledge becomes more valuable when we can use it. But many education systems teach us to be afraid of *testing* ideas. If we question something, experiment with it, or temporarily doubt it to see whether it works (and through that we actually learn and understand the knowledge), we may be treated as a "bad student".

But the thing is, to put knowledge into practice, we need an environment where people are not afraid to try. the cost of trying and failing should be low - so low that failure does not feel embarrassing, permanent, or dangerous. When experimentation feels safe, people are more willing to explore.

By trying something, whether it works or not, we move closer to understand it:
- we discover what we do not yet understand.
- we receive feedback that show us where our thinking is wrong. (it could be outside or inside)
- we can adjust our approach/understanding and try again.

By "playable", i mean that we should be able to **play with knowledge**. We should be able to test it ourselves, revisit what we have learned, and see whether it actually works. Instead of only receiving information, we should be able to interact with it.

Learning should not only be informative. It should also be exploratory, enjoyable, and safe enough to allow failure.
